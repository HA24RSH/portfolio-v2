# Ship Fast, Iterate Faster

The most impactful habit I've developed as an engineer is simple: **ship early, ship often**.

Not because the first version is good. It usually isn't. But because shipping forces you to confront reality — and reality is the fastest teacher.

## Why "Done" Beats "Perfect"

When I started building projects, I'd polish every edge case before showing anyone. I'd spend weeks on code that nobody ended up using. Then one day, I shipped a rough prototype and got more useful feedback in 24 hours than I had in 3 weeks of solo refinement.

The lesson? **Users don't care about your internal standards. They care about solving their problem.**

## The Feedback Loop

```
Build → Ship → Measure → Learn → Build again
```

Each loop is a chance to course-correct. The faster you complete loops, the faster you grow.

Here's what shipping early has given me:

1. **Real feedback** over assumed requirements
2. **Momentum** — nothing beats the energy of something going live
3. **Learning** — you discover edge cases only real usage reveals
4. **Confidence** — every ship is proof you can finish

## Practical Tips

- **Set a ship date before you start.** Deadlines force decisions.
- **Define "done" as "useful" not "perfect."** A working 80% beats an unreleased 100%.
- **Use feature flags** to ship incomplete features safely.
- **Share early** with one trusted user before the world.

## The Mindset Shift

Perfectionism feels noble. But it's often fear in disguise — fear of criticism, of failure, of not being good enough.

Shipping is the antidote. Every release, no matter how small, is a vote for action over fear.

Start. Ship. Iterate. Repeat.

# The Overthinking Trap

I used to think that if I just planned *a little more*, researched *a little longer*, or waited until conditions were *just right*, I'd finally be ready to start.

I was wrong.

## The Trap

Overthinking is disguised as preparation. It feels productive — you're busy thinking, planning, weighing options. But most of the time, you're just delaying.

> "You don't have to be great to start, but you have to start to be great."
> — Zig Ziglar

Here's what overthinking actually costs you:

- **Time** — hours spent in your head that could have been spent building
- **Confidence** — the more you overthink, the more doubt grows
- **Momentum** — action builds momentum; overthinking kills it

## What I Do Instead

I picked up a simple rule: **if a decision takes less than 5 minutes to execute, just do it**.

For bigger things, I set a **decision deadline**. I give myself a fixed amount of time to research, then I commit — even if I'm not 100% sure.

```bash
# A simple daily ritual I follow
git commit -m "ship it"
```

## The Bottom Line

The best way to think less is to **act more**. Start small, iterate fast, and let reality be your feedback loop — not your imagination.

The overthinking trap is comfortable because it feels safe. But growth lives outside that comfort zone.

So close this tab, open your editor, and start.
